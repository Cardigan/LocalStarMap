A MAP, NOT A BACKDROP

Real space. Honest scale.

This map uses your supplied reference\50lys-list.txt: 132 nearby systems, plus Sol, within 50 light-years of Earth. It is a bright-star selection (Earth apparent magnitude 6.5 or brighter), not a complete census. Apart from the requested direction references below, faint red dwarfs and distant sky stars aren't added.

Seven additional stars requested as direction references are outside that 50-light-year catalog: Hadar, Polaris, CS Camelopardalis, Miram, Alpha Lupi, Alpha Circini, and Delta Circini. Their J2000 positions, visual magnitudes, and parallaxes come from SIMBAD / CDS (retrieved October 23, 2026); approximate distances use inverse parallax. These distant references are labeled separately in the catalog.

Direction names preserve your reference views: from Earth facing Alpha Centauri, or from Alpha Centauri facing Sol, with celestial north up and east left. The labels do not change names when you turn; their arrows track the actual star direction from the current camera. They are not Earth's geographic compass directions. Select a marker to look at it; double-click a star to travel.

J2000 right ascension, declination, and distance are converted into fixed 3D Cartesian positions in light-years. Moving the observer changes every star's direction, distance, and displayed brightness. Stars are not attached to a sky sphere.

Earth is represented by Sol at this scale. Multiple stars share one catalog system position; Alpha Centauri's components, including Proxima, are not separately resolved. The endpoint is the catalog system position.

Click Neighborhood to switch to an external, orbitable 3D map inspired by your reference image. Sol-centered distance rings lie in the Galactic reference plane, and vertical guides show each star's height above or below it. Drag to orbit, scroll to change camera distance, and use Sky view to return without moving the observer. Selecting a star centers the external camera on it; double-clicking starts a journey in sky view.

Point sizes and glow are enhanced for readability. Grid lines are guides, not physical objects. The small overview is a fixed equatorial projection, while the external map uses a Galactic frame. Travel is cinematic, not a simulation of physically possible speed, light-time, proper motion, or relativistic effects.

Reference provenance: Hipparcos (1997), Harvard Revised Bright Star Catalogue (1991), and Gliese / Jahreiß (1991), as cited in your supplied file. Positions and distances are preserved as supplied, not updated measurements.
